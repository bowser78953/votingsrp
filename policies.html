SECTION I — PURPOSE AND SCOPE

1.1 Purpose

The purpose of the Springfield Roleplay (SRP) Election Rules & Policies is to establish a comprehensive, fair, transparent, and professionally administered election system for all governmental elections conducted within Springfield Roleplay.

These regulations are designed to ensure that every election is conducted with integrity, impartiality, accountability, and equal opportunity for all eligible candidates and voters. The election process shall prioritize qualifications, leadership ability, community contribution, professional conduct, and demonstrated merit rather than popularity, personal influence, or outside political interests.

The election system is intended to provide an organized environment in which individuals can seek public office, present their qualifications, demonstrate their leadership capabilities, and earn the confidence of the community through appropriate participation.

1.2 Objectives

The objectives of these policies are to:

  1. Establish consistent election procedures and standards.
  2. Protect the integrity and credibility of all SRP elections.
  3. Ensure equal treatment and opportunity for every eligible candidate.
  4. Prevent harassment, intimidation, manipulation, and election fraud.
  5. Maintain strict neutrality throughout the election process.
  6. Prohibit real-world political influence and political activity.
  7. Promote responsible campaigning and informed voting.
  8. Ensure that elected individuals demonstrate the qualifications necessary to serve.
  9. Establish clear enforcement procedures and disciplinary consequences.
  10. Maintain public confidence in Springfield Roleplay's governmental institutions.

1.3 Scope of Application

These regulations apply to all individuals participating in or associated with an SRP election, including:

  • Candidates seeking elected government positions.
  • Registered voters participating in elections.
  • Election Officials and election administration personnel.
  • Government officials involved in election proceedings.
  • Campaign representatives and authorized campaign personnel.
  • Community members participating in election-related discussions.
  • Any individual attempting to influence, interfere with, or manipulate an election.

Participation in an SRP election constitutes agreement to comply with these regulations.

1.4 Authority

The designated SRP election administration authority shall oversee the implementation, interpretation, and enforcement of these policies. No candidate, voter, government official, or campaign representative shall claim exemption from these regulations based on their position, rank, seniority, or personal relationship with another individual.

SECTION II — DEFINITIONS

For the purposes of these regulations, the following definitions shall apply:

2.1 Candidate

An individual who has formally registered, qualified, and been approved to seek an elected position within Springfield Roleplay.

2.2 Election Official

An authorized individual responsible for administering, monitoring, supervising, or enforcing election procedures. Election Officials must remain impartial and shall not use their authority to favor or disadvantage any candidate.

2.3 Voter

An eligible member of Springfield Roleplay who has been granted authorization to participate in an official election and submit a valid ballot.

2.4 Campaigning

Any permitted activity conducted by a candidate or their authorized representatives for the purpose of presenting qualifications, leadership experience, plans, and intentions to voters.

2.5 Political Content

Any real-world political material, including references to political parties, ideologies, movements, political figures, government officials, political campaigns, endorsements, propaganda, or real-world political disputes.

2.6 Election Misconduct

Any action that violates these regulations, interferes with election fairness, manipulates voting outcomes, compromises election integrity, or creates an unfair advantage for a candidate.

2.7 Campaign Representative

An individual authorized by a candidate to assist with permitted campaign activities, provided that such individual complies with all applicable election regulations.

2.8 Election Period

The officially designated timeframe beginning with the announcement or opening of an election and ending when the election administration authority formally declares the results and concludes the election process.

2.9 Vote Manipulation

Any intentional attempt to improperly influence, alter, fabricate, purchase, pressure, or otherwise interfere with the voting decisions or results of an election.

SECTION III — ELECTION CONDUCT STANDARDS

3.1 Professional Conduct

All candidates, voters, Election Officials, and participants shall maintain professional conduct throughout the election period.

Individuals are expected to:

  1. Communicate respectfully and appropriately.
  2. Follow all instructions issued by authorized Election Officials.
  3. Avoid unnecessary arguments, hostility, or disruptive behavior.
  4. Respect the rights and participation of other candidates and voters.
  5. Maintain professionalism in all election-related communications.
  6. Refrain from conduct that damages the credibility of the election process.

3.2 Equal Treatment

All candidates shall receive equal treatment regardless of rank, department, tenure, personal relationships, popularity, or previous government experience.

Election Officials shall not:

  • Provide preferential treatment to specific candidates.
  • Restrict another candidate's permitted campaign activities unfairly.
  • Manipulate election procedures to benefit an individual.
  • Disclose confidential election information to unauthorized individuals.
  • Use their position to influence the outcome of an election.

3.3 Anti-Harassment Policy

Harassment, threats, discrimination, targeted humiliation, and intimidation directed toward candidates, voters, or election personnel are strictly prohibited.

Disagreements concerning candidate qualifications or campaign proposals must remain respectful and relevant to the election.

3.4 Candidate Equality

Every approved candidate shall receive the same applicable campaign opportunities, access to official election announcements, and consideration under these policies.

No candidate shall be disadvantaged because another candidate possesses greater popularity, more personal connections, or a higher position within the government.

SECTION IV — POLITICAL NEUTRALITY POLICY

STRICTLY ENFORCED

4.1 General Prohibition

Springfield Roleplay maintains a strictly non-political election environment.

Real-world political discussion, advocacy, campaigning, endorsements, ideological promotion, and political influence are prohibited throughout the entire election process.

SRP elections exist exclusively for the purposes of fictional governmental roleplay, community representation, leadership selection, and organizational development.

Real-world political activity shall not be introduced into, associated with, or used to influence any SRP election.

4.2 Prohibited Political Activity

The following activities are strictly prohibited:

  1. Promoting or advocating for real-world political parties.
  2. Supporting or opposing real-world political candidates or elected officials.
  3. Discussing or debating real-world political ideologies.
  4. Promoting real-world political movements or organizations.
  5. Using real-world political endorsements to influence voters.
  6. Referencing real-world political campaigns or elections as a means of persuasion.
  7. Distributing political propaganda or campaign material.
  8. Using real-world political slogans, symbols, flags, or imagery for political promotion.
  9. Attempting to associate an SRP candidate with a real-world political movement.
  10. Using real-world political controversies to attack or discredit another candidate.

4.3 Political Endorsements

Candidates and campaign representatives shall not seek, publish, distribute, or use endorsements from real-world political figures, parties, organizations, or movements.

No individual may use real-world political affiliation or perceived political support as a qualification for public office within SRP.

4.4 Political Debates

Real-world political debates are prohibited in:

  • Official election channels.
  • Candidate campaign channels.
  • Public election discussions.
  • Candidate speeches and presentations.
  • Election-related community events.
  • Any other designated election communication platform.

4.5 Political Symbolism and Propaganda

Candidates shall not incorporate real-world political propaganda, party insignia, ideological symbols, political slogans, or political promotional materials into their campaigns.

4.7 Enforcement

Any violation of this section shall be subject to investigation and disciplinary action. Depending on severity, penalties may include campaign suspension, candidate disqualification, or permanent election participation restrictions.

Ignorance of this policy shall not automatically excuse a violation.

SECTION V — CAMPAIGN RULES

5.1 Authorized Campaigning

Candidates may campaign only through approved communication methods and within the officially designated election period.

Permitted campaign activities include:

  1. Publishing candidate introductions.
  2. Presenting qualifications and previous experience.
  3. Explaining leadership objectives and goals.
  4. Hosting approved campaign presentations.
  5. Participating in official candidate discussions.
  6. Responding to relevant voter questions.
  7. Publishing proposals concerning Springfield Roleplay.
  8. Explaining how they intend to fulfill the responsibilities of the office.

5.2 Prohibited Campaign Activities

The following activities are prohibited:

  1. Harassing or threatening opposing candidates.
  2. Spreading knowingly false information about opponents.
  3. Impersonating another candidate or Election Official.
  4. Publishing fabricated endorsements or qualifications.
  5. Offering rewards in exchange for votes.
  6. Buying, selling, or trading votes.
  7. Pressuring individuals to vote for a particular candidate.
  8. Manipulating ballots or election results.
  9. Using unauthorized accounts to influence voting.
  10. Publishing confidential election information.
  11. Conducting unauthorized mass advertisements or campaign distributions.
  12. Using staff authority or departmental rank to pressure voters.

5.3 Mass Messaging Restrictions

Candidates and campaign representatives shall not engage in excessive messaging or unsolicited campaign distribution.

This includes:

  • Repeatedly tagging or mentioning community members.
  • Sending unsolicited campaign advertisements through direct messages.
  • Mass-pinging server members.
  • Spamming election channels.
  • Using bots or automated accounts to distribute campaign material.
  • Repeatedly contacting individuals after they have requested that campaigning stop.

Campaign announcements must remain within designated campaign channels or other locations specifically authorized by Election Officials.

5.4 Vote Buying and Bribery

The exchange of money, in-game currency, privileges, positions, promotions, rewards, or other benefits in return for votes is strictly prohibited.

Candidates shall not:

  • Promise government appointments in exchange for votes.
  • Offer promotions or special treatment for electoral support.
  • Provide rewards for ballot submissions.
  • Threaten to remove privileges because of voting preferences.

Any confirmed attempt to purchase, exchange, or improperly influence votes may result in immediate candidate disqualification.

5.5 Truthfulness and Accuracy

All campaign statements must be truthful, accurate, and reasonably verifiable.

Candidates shall not fabricate:

  • Previous government experience.
  • Departmental accomplishments.
  • Qualifications or certifications.
  • Official endorsements.
  • Election statistics.
  • Governmental authority or responsibilities.

Candidates must distinguish between established facts and proposed future objectives.

5.6 Campaign Period

All campaigning must occur within the timeframe established by the election administration authority.

Campaigning before the official opening or after the designated closing time is prohibited unless expressly authorized.

SECTION VI — CANDIDATE RESPONSIBILITIES

6.1 General Responsibilities

Every candidate is responsible for understanding and complying with these regulations.

Candidates shall:

  1. Provide accurate registration information.
  2. Meet all eligibility requirements.
  3. Maintain appropriate conduct throughout the election.
  4. Respect Election Officials and their lawful instructions.
  5. Present qualifications honestly.
  6. Refrain from prohibited political activity.
  7. Respect opposing candidates.
  8. Cooperate with election investigations.
  9. Avoid actions that undermine election integrity.
  10. Accept the established election results and procedures, subject to the appeals process.

6.2 Ethical Campaigning

Candidates must campaign based on their own qualifications, leadership abilities, proposed contributions, and understanding of the position.

Campaigning shall not rely on harassment, intimidation, personal attacks, fabricated information, or improper influence.

6.3 Respect for Opponents

Candidates may disagree with opposing proposals but must not engage in personal attacks or targeted harassment.

Permitted example:

A candidate explains why their proposed departmental reform would address an issue differently.

Prohibited example:

A candidate publicly humiliates another candidate or spreads fabricated allegations to damage their reputation.

6.4 Accountability

Candidates remain responsible for actions undertaken by their authorized campaign representatives.

A candidate may be subject to disciplinary action when they knowingly authorize, encourage, coordinate, or benefit from misconduct committed on their behalf.

6.5 Withdrawal

Candidates may request withdrawal from an election through the designated election administration authority.

Withdrawal requests must be formally submitted and acknowledged. Candidates who withdraw may not continue campaigning as active candidates.

SECTION VII — VOTER RESPONSIBILITIES

7.1 Independent Voting

Every eligible voter is entitled to make an independent voting decision without harassment, coercion, intimidation, or improper influence.

Voters are encouraged to evaluate candidates based on:

  • Qualifications and experience.
  • Leadership ability.
  • Previous contributions to Springfield Roleplay.
  • Professional conduct.
  • Understanding of governmental responsibilities.
  • Demonstrated reliability and activity.
  • Ability to fulfill the duties of the position.

7.2 Voting Integrity

Voters shall not:

  1. Submit multiple ballots when only one is permitted.
  2. Impersonate another voter.
  3. Submit fraudulent voting information.
  4. Sell or exchange their vote.
  5. Accept rewards in exchange for electoral support.
  6. Manipulate or interfere with another individual's ballot.
  7. Coordinate fraudulent voting schemes.

7.3 Respectful Participation

Voters must respect other members' decisions and shall not harass, threaten, or publicly target individuals based on their voting preferences.

7.4 Reporting Misconduct

Voters are encouraged to report suspected violations to authorized Election Officials through the designated reporting procedures.

Reports should include relevant information and available evidence.

7.5 Confidentiality

Voters are not required to publicly disclose their individual voting choices unless an officially established election procedure specifically requires relevant information for administrative purposes.

SECTION VIII — ENFORCEMENT AND VIOLATIONS

8.1 Enforcement Authority

The designated SRP election administration authority shall be responsible for investigating, reviewing, and addressing alleged violations of these regulations.

Election Officials shall exercise their authority impartially and consistently.

No individual shall be exempt from investigation because of government rank, position, seniority, or personal relationships.

8.2 Reporting Procedures

Suspected violations may be reported through an official Election Administration or Government Management ticket.

Reports should include:

  1. The name or username of the individual involved.
  2. A description of the suspected violation.
  3. The approximate date and time of the incident.
  4. Relevant screenshots, recordings, or message links.
  5. Names of witnesses, where applicable.
  6. Any additional information that may assist the investigation.

Knowingly submitting fabricated reports or deliberately misleading Election Officials is itself an offense.

8.3 Investigation Procedures

Upon receiving a report, Election Officials may:

  1. Review the submitted evidence.
  2. Request additional documentation.
  3. Interview relevant individuals.
  4. Review campaign communications.
  5. Examine voting records where authorized.
  6. Temporarily restrict campaign activity when necessary to protect election integrity.
  7. Submit findings to the appropriate reviewing authority.

All investigations must be conducted fairly and without favoritism.

8.4 Evidence Standards

Disciplinary decisions must be based on relevant and reasonably reliable evidence.

Acceptable evidence may include:

  • Screenshots of Discord messages.
  • Message links and timestamps.
  • Recorded election-related communications.
  • Official voting records.
  • Statements from credible witnesses.
  • Relevant administrative logs.

Unverified accusations, personal disagreements, rumors, and unsupported allegations shall not independently establish misconduct.

Evidence must be reviewed in context, including relevant surrounding communications when available.

8.5 Classification of Violations

Violations shall be classified according to their severity, intent, frequency, and impact on election integrity.

Classification

Description

Level I — Minor

Accidental procedural violations or minor campaign rule violations.

Level II — Moderate

Repeated misconduct, disruptive campaigning, or failure to follow official instructions.

Level III — Serious

Harassment, intimidation, deliberate misinformation, or unauthorized political promotion.

Level IV — Severe

Vote buying, ballot manipulation, organized political influence, bribery, or deliberate election interference.

8.6 Investigation Impartiality

Any Election Official who has a personal, professional, campaign, or other material conflict of interest in a case must disclose that conflict and recuse themselves from the investigation or decision.

A replacement reviewer shall be appointed where necessary.

SECTION IX — PENALTIES FOR POLITICAL VIOLATIONS

ZERO TOLERANCE FOR ELECTION MANIPULATION

9.1 General Penalty Authority

The election administration authority may impose disciplinary measures based on the severity of the violation, the individual's intent, prior disciplinary history, and the impact on the election.

Penalties shall be proportionate to the offense. The warning system does not prevent immediate disqualification when a serious violation warrants it.

9.2 Warning System

The following disciplinary structure shall generally apply to minor and moderate violations:

First Offense — Official Warning

  • Formal notification of the violation.
  • Explanation of the applicable regulation.
  • Requirement to correct the misconduct.
  • Documentation of the warning.

Second Offense — Final Warning

  • Formal final warning.
  • Possible temporary campaign restrictions.
  • Increased administrative monitoring.
  • Documentation of repeated misconduct.

Third Offense — Campaign Suspension

  • Temporary suspension of campaign privileges.
  • Removal of unauthorized campaign material.
  • Possible referral for additional disciplinary review.

9.3 Candidate Disqualification

A candidate may be disqualified for:

  • Serious or repeated violations of election regulations.
  • Deliberate political advocacy or propaganda.
  • Vote buying or bribery.
  • Threatening or intimidating voters.
  • Fabricating qualifications or official endorsements.
  • Manipulating ballots or election outcomes.
  • Coordinating organized election interference.
  • Refusing to comply with lawful election administration decisions.

Disqualification must be supported by documented findings and an official decision.

9.4 Temporary Participation Restrictions

Individuals may be temporarily restricted from:

  • Campaigning.
  • Participating in official election discussions.
  • Holding campaign representative privileges.
  • Participating in specific election activities.

Restrictions must be limited to what is reasonably necessary and authorized under SRP administrative procedures.

9.5 Permanent Election Bans

Permanent election participation bans may be imposed in cases involving severe or repeated misconduct, particularly where an individual deliberately compromises election integrity.

Such bans may apply to:

  • Candidates.
  • Campaign representatives.
  • Individuals involved in organized vote manipulation.
  • Individuals repeatedly engaging in prohibited political influence.
  • Individuals committing serious election fraud.

Permanent bans must be reviewed and formally approved by the designated authority.

9.6 Election Annulment

Where substantial evidence establishes that misconduct materially compromised the validity or fairness of an election, the authorized government administration may declare the election invalid and order a new election.

Any such decision must include documented reasons and appropriate administrative authorization.

SECTION X — APPEALS PROCESS

10.1 Appeal Eligibility

Any candidate or participant who receives a formal disciplinary action under these regulations may submit an appeal.

Appeals must challenge the decision based on one or more of the following grounds:

  1. Relevant evidence was overlooked.
  2. The decision was based on inaccurate information.
  3. The applicable regulations were incorrectly interpreted.
  4. The disciplinary measure was disproportionate to the established violation.
  5. A conflict of interest compromised the investigation.
  6. The individual was not provided a reasonable opportunity to respond.

Disagreement with an unfavorable outcome, without a substantive appeal ground, shall not automatically justify reconsideration.

10.2 Appeal Submission

Appeals must be submitted through the designated SRP Government Management or Election Administration ticket system.

An appeal should include:

  • The individual's username.
  • The original disciplinary decision.
  • The reason for the appeal.
  • Supporting evidence.
  • Any relevant additional explanation.

10.3 Review Procedures

Appeals shall be reviewed by an authorized individual who was not materially involved in the original decision, where reasonably possible.

The reviewing authority may:

  1. Uphold the original decision.
  2. Reduce the disciplinary penalty.
  3. Reverse the disciplinary decision.
  4. Request additional evidence.
  5. Order a new investigation.
  6. Refer the matter to higher government administration.

10.4 Final Decisions

All appeal decisions must be documented and communicated to the individual concerned.

The final determination of an appeal shall rest with the designated SRP authority under the established government hierarchy.

Filing an appeal does not automatically suspend an existing disciplinary action unless the reviewing authority expressly orders otherwise.

SECTION XI — ELECTION INTEGRITY AND FAIRNESS MEASURES

11.1 Transparency

Election administration shall maintain clear and accessible procedures.

The following information should be communicated to the community:

  • Election announcements and schedules.
  • Candidate registration requirements.
  • Candidate eligibility standards.
  • Campaign rules and restrictions.
  • Voting procedures.
  • Election deadlines.
  • Official election results.
  • Relevant administrative decisions.

Sensitive personal information and confidential ballots shall remain protected.

11.2 Anti-Manipulation Measures

The election administration authority shall implement reasonable safeguards to prevent:

  1. Duplicate voting.
  2. Unauthorized ballot submissions.
  3. Fake or alternate account voting.
  4. Ballot tampering.
  5. Unauthorized access to election records.
  6. Coordinated voter manipulation.
  7. Unauthorized alteration of election results.
  8. Misrepresentation of official election information.

11.3 Prevention of Voter Influence Schemes

No candidate, government official, or campaign representative may use their authority, personal connections, departmental position, or administrative privileges to improperly influence voters.

Government departments and leadership positions shall not be used as instruments for electoral pressure.

11.4 Impartial Election Administration

All Election Officials must remain neutral throughout the election period.

Election Officials shall not:

  • Publicly endorse candidates in their official capacity.
  • Manipulate candidate eligibility decisions.
  • Interfere with legitimate voting decisions.
  • Disclose confidential ballots.
  • Alter election results without proper authorization.
  • Use administrative permissions to disadvantage a candidate.

11.5 Candidate Qualification Standards

Elections shall be merit-based and focused on an individual's ability to perform the responsibilities associated with the position.

Candidates may be evaluated using the following considerations:

QualificationEvaluation Focus
Leadership AbilityAbility to lead, organize, and make responsible decisions.
ExperienceRelevant previous government or community experience.

No candidate shall be guaranteed an electoral outcome based solely on popularity, personal relationships, or seniority.

11.6 Election Result Verification

Before results are officially announced, Election Officials shall verify the submitted ballots and confirm that the reported outcome accurately reflects the valid votes received.

Any discrepancies must be investigated before certification.

11.7 Official Results

Only authorized Election Officials may formally certify and announce election results.

Once certified, results shall be published through an official SRP government communication channel.

11.8 Conflicts of Interest

Candidates and Election Officials must disclose any relevant conflict of interest that could compromise their responsibilities.

Individuals involved in such conflicts may be required to recuse themselves from specific election-related decisions.

SECTION XII — FINAL POLICY STATEMENT

12.1 Official Declaration

The Springfield Roleplay Election Rules & Policies establish the mandatory standards governing all elections conducted within Springfield Roleplay.

Every election shall be administered with fairness, transparency, neutrality, professionalism, and accountability. The integrity of the electoral process shall remain a priority, and no individual shall be permitted to compromise that integrity through misconduct, manipulation, intimidation, or improper influence.

All elections within Springfield Roleplay shall be based exclusively on qualifications, leadership ability, community contribution, professional conduct, experience, responsibility, and demonstrated merit.

Real-world political activity has absolutely no place within the SRP election process. Political parties, political ideologies, political figures, political movements, political endorsements, propaganda, and real-world political disputes shall not be introduced or promoted during any election.

Candidates must earn the confidence of the community through their own abilities, conduct, experience, and commitment to Springfield Roleplay. Voters must be afforded the opportunity to make independent decisions without pressure, intimidation, or manipulation.

Election Officials shall uphold these regulations consistently and without favoritism, ensuring that every candidate receives equal consideration and every valid vote is treated with integrity.

12.2 Mandatory Compliance

Compliance with these regulations is mandatory for all candidates, voters, Election Officials, campaign representatives, and other individuals participating in an SRP election.

Failure to comply may result in disciplinary action, including warnings, campaign suspensions, candidate disqualification, temporary restrictions, or permanent election participation bans.

The designated SRP government authority reserves the right to interpret, administer, and amend these regulations when necessary to preserve election integrity and community stability.

12.3 Governing Principle

SPRINGFIELD ROLEPLAY

ONE COMMUNITY. ONE STANDARD. FAIR ELECTIONS.

OFFICIAL SRP ELECTION PRINCIPLE

By participating in any Springfield Roleplay election, all individuals acknowledge that the election process is a privilege governed by these regulations and agree to uphold the standards established herein.
